About Me

Where Curiosity Started

I've always been interested in understanding how things work, why they change, and how seemingly unrelated ideas connect. At the University of Minnesota, I pursued two very different fields: computer science and history, with a focus on prehistoric Europe. History taught me to investigate patterns, understand complex systems, and consider how people and societies evolve. Computer science gave me the tools to apply that curiosity by designing and building things of my own.

Building Software

I found myself drawn to software engineering because it combines analytical problem-solving with practical creativity. I enjoy working across the stack, understanding how different parts of an application fit together, and turning complicated requirements into solutions people can actually use. My work has exposed me to everything from frontend development and backend architecture to database optimization and production troubleshooting. I'm particularly interested in building thoughtful, reliable software that solves meaningful operational problems rather than simply adding more features.

Life on the Water

Outside of technology, competitive rowing has been a defining part of my life for more than a decade. As a coxswain, I've spent years navigating difficult courses, making decisions in real time, and coordinating crews through demanding races. It's a role that requires equal parts communication, strategy, technical awareness, and the ability to stay composed when circumstances change quickly.

A Defining Race

One of my proudest moments came at the Head of the Charles Regatta, where my crew passed seven boats on the way to earning a medal. Beyond the result itself, the race reinforced something I've carried into every part of my life: good decisions aren't always obvious, conditions rarely go exactly as planned, and staying calm enough to adapt can make all the difference.

How I Approach My Work

Whether I'm investigating a production issue, designing a new feature, or working through an ambiguous problem with a team, I bring the same approach: stay curious, understand the bigger picture, communicate clearly, and be intentional about the decisions I make. My background has taught me to look beyond the immediate problem and consider the people, systems, and processes connected to it. I believe the best engineering happens when technical decisions are grounded in a real understanding of the problems they're meant to solve.
